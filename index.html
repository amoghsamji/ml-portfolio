Spaceship Titanic: A Machine Learning Deep Dive

A comprehensive walkthrough of my journey scaling the Kaggle Leaderboard to 81.4% Accuracy.

Domain Understanding

The Spaceship Titanic was an interstellar passenger liner launched in 2912. During its voyage to transport roughly 13,000 emigrants from our solar system to three newly habitable exoplanets, it collided with a spacetime anomaly hidden within a dust cloud.

Though the ship remained intact, almost half of the passengers were mysteriously transported to an alternate dimension. The core objective of this predictive modeling project is to rescue the lost passengers by predicting exactly which individuals were transported by the anomaly using records recovered from the spaceship's damaged computer system.

Understanding the physical constraints of the ship (e.g., specific decks correspond to specific planetary origins, and VIP status correlates to certain spending habits) is critical to building a high-performing model.

Data Preprocessing & Deterministic Imputation

The raw data was heavily riddled with missing values (NaNs). Instead of using blind statistical imputation (like simple means/medians), I implemented Deterministic Imputation—using the strict physical rules of the spaceship to perfectly fill missing data:

  • The CryoSleep Rule: If a passenger's Total Spend across all amenities was exactly 0.0, it is a near-absolute certainty they were in CryoSleep. Missing CryoSleep values were filled logically using this boolean flag.
  • Relational Mapping: Missing HomePlanet or Destination values were back-filled by mapping them to other passengers sharing the same Group_Id (extracted from PassengerId) or Surname. Families travel together.
  • Physical Ship Constraints: Passengers on Decks A, B, C, or T belonged strictly to Europa. Deck G belonged strictly to Earth. Missing origins were imputed based on their Cabin location.

Analytical Insights & Data Relationships

Before throwing algorithms at the data, analyzing feature correlations revealed several "Grandmaster-level" insights that drove the feature engineering process:

  • The Zero-Spend Phenomenon: Plotting the distribution of the target variable against financial features revealed that passengers with zero total spend were overwhelmingly "Transported". This perfectly correlated with the CryoSleep status.
  • Family Survival Rates: Analysis of the PassengerId prefix (Group ID) showed that survival/transportation was not randomly distributed. Entire family groups tended to share the exact same fate.
  • Cabin Side Impact: Parsing the cabin strings showed that passengers on the Starboard (S) vs. Port (P) side of the ship experienced different anomaly impacts.
  • Age Brackets: Passengers under the age of 13 had distinct survival rates compared to adults, indicating emergency priority protocols for children on the ship.

Feature Engineering & Selection

Models are only as good as the data they consume. I created a robust data pipeline to extract hidden signals from the raw strings:

  • TotalSpend: Aggregated RoomService + FoodCourt + ShoppingMall + Spa + VRDeck. This single metric drastically reduced noise across the 5 financial columns.
  • Cabin Parsing: Created a custom function to divide the Cabin column into CabinDeck, CabinNum, and CabinSide.
  • Name Extraction: Extracted the Surname to map family relationships mathematically.

Strategic Feature Selection (Pruning):

To break past the 81% accuracy ceiling, I realized certain features were causing Tree-based models to overfit the training data. I explicitly dropped noisy columns (like Age, specific Destinations, and VIP statuses) before the final training phase:

Model Building & Evolution

The architecture evolved significantly over weeks of experimentation to push the boundaries of accuracy.

Phase 1: The 80.7% Diverse Ensemble

Started with a Soft-Voting Ensemble prioritizing mathematical diversity to cancel out uncorrelated errors. It combined LightGBM (Tree-based), an MLP Classifier (Neural Net), and an SVC (Geometric mapping).

Phase 2: High-Cardinality Experiments

Attempted to use Native CatBoost (passing raw strings) to handle high-cardinality features like Surname without one-hot encoding, combined with a 5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation strategy. Also tested Gaussian Noise Injection to double the dataset, which proved that tabular decision boundaries in this dataset require strict precision, not synthetic variance.

Phase 3: The Final XGBoost + SMOTE Architecture

The ultimate breakthrough came from simplifying the ensemble and focusing heavily on class balancing and regularization. I applied SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) to perfectly balance the data, combined with a highly tuned XGBoost Classifier.

params_XGB = { 'learning_rate': 0.06672, 'n_estimators': 728, 'max_depth': 5, 'lambda': 3.05, # Heavy L2 Regularization 'alpha': 4.59, # Heavy L1 Regularization 'colsample_bytree': 0.924, 'subsample': 0.953 } model = xgb.XGBClassifier(**params_XGB)

Why This Architecture Outperformed the Rest:

  • Immunity to Noise vs. Neural Nets: The previous ensemble relied heavily on a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and an SVM. Both of these are notoriously sensitive to extreme financial outliers. Even with a RobustScaler, the variance in TotalSpend caused the neural network to memorize noise rather than broad survival patterns.
  • Handling Minority Boundaries: While CatBoost was excellent at parsing categorical strings, it struggled to define clear decision boundaries in the slightly imbalanced subsets of the data. SMOTE explicitly generated synthetic neighbors for the minority class, forcing XGBoost to learn exactly where the boundary was.
  • The Power of Pruning & Penalty: Tree models normally overfit synthetic data. However, by dropping noisy columns (like Age) and heavily turning up XGBoost's L1/L2 penalties (alpha=4.59, lambda=3.05) alongside a shallow tree depth (max_depth=5), the model was forced to generalize. It learned the true signal of the spaceship's disaster instead of memorizing individual synthetic passengers.

Conclusion & Key Takeaways

This project was a masterclass in the reality of tabular data science. The biggest lessons learned were:

  • Feature Engineering > Model Complexity: A single, well-thought-out logical rule (like Deterministic Imputation based on physical ship layout) provided a higher accuracy baseline than simply stacking more neural networks.
  • Less is More (Strategic Pruning): Dropping features (like Age and specific One-Hot targets) actually increased accuracy. Sometimes, providing an algorithm with less data prevents it from finding false correlations.
  • The SMOTE + Regularization Synergy: SMOTE is incredibly powerful for tabular data, but it is dangerous on its own. Pairing it with XGBoost's harsh L1/L2 regularization created the perfect balance of synthetic learning and strict generalization.
