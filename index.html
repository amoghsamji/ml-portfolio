Featured Project: Spaceship Titanic

Predicting Interdimensional Transport

Based on the renowned Kaggle competition, the goal of this project was to predict whether passengers on the Spaceship Titanic were transported to an alternate dimension during a spacetime anomaly. This required analyzing complex, high-cardinality tabular data.

Extracting Hidden Signals

The raw dataset required extensive preprocessing. I implemented advanced feature engineering to expose the underlying patterns of survival:

  • Spending Metrics: Aggregated RoomService, FoodCourt, ShoppingMall, Spa, and VRDeck into a single TotalSpend feature.
  • Deterministic Imputation: Built physical logic rules (e.g., if a passenger had a TotalSpend of 0, they were flagged as being in CryoSleep).
  • Cabin Parsing: Split the complex Cabin string into structural components (Deck, Num, Side) to map physical ship locations.
  • Relational Dynamics: Extracted family groups and surnames to track survival correlations among passengers traveling together.

The Journey to 81.4%

Breaking the 80% accuracy ceiling required multiple iterations and aggressive experimentation:

Phase 1: The 80.7% Diverse Ensemble

I started with a Soft-Voting Ensemble combining mathematically diverse models to cancel out individual weaknesses:

  • Tree-Based: LightGBM
  • Neural Network: Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)
  • Geometric: Support Vector Machine (SVM)

While powerful, this ensemble struggled with extreme spending outliers and highly categorical variables.

Phase 2: Experimentation & Overfitting

I attempted to use Gaussian Noise Injection to create synthetic data and expand the training set to over 17,000 rows. However, this blurred the logical decision boundaries. I also experimented with Native CatBoost to handle categorical text without One-Hot Encoding, which provided great insights but wasn't the final key.

The Winning Architecture

To achieve the final 81.4% accuracy, I pivoted to a highly optimized XGBoost pipeline coupled with advanced class balancing.

  • Handling Imbalance: Integrated SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) to perfectly balance the binary target classes without blurring continuous boundaries.
  • Clean Encoding: Utilized SimpleImputer for missing values and strict OneHotEncoder to convert remaining categoricals into machine-readable formats.
  • Feature Pruning: Strategically dropped noisy columns (like Age, specific Destinations, and VIP statuses) that were causing the trees to overfit.
  • Hyperparameter Tuning: Deployed XGBoost with customized parameters (learning_rate: 0.066, max_depth: 5, alpha: 4.59) to force strong generalization on unseen test data.

This final streamlined, regularized approach proved far superior to the heavy ensembles, proving that clean data processing and targeted boosting beat raw complexity.
